Short Story

The Editorial Adventures of Edith and Harold Lettermice

Eric Davidson

Some stories are worth telling slowly.

I've been fortunate enough to meet Harold and Edith, and after quite a lot of begging, I've convinced them to go into business together. They would like to keep their financial goals private for now, but may I say that they are far more ambitious than you would expect for a pair so small.

This is a snail-mail club. Every month, you stand to receive a variety of written pieces, composed in collaboration by myself, Harold, and Edith.

Okay, in truth, it will mostly be me, but much of it will be about them.

"A 'snail mail' club? Why not mice mail?" asked Harold.

"I knew this much attention wasn't good for you," said Edith, "That's simply what they call it. You didn't invite writing and you certainly didn't invent the mail."

"Well," said Harold.

I think their story is worth telling, one small piece at a time, and it feels like it should come through the mail. In addition, I plan to include (should they allow) examples of romantic letters they have revised as well as their notes (disagreements) on the process. Of far less interest, I would also like to share my own thoughts about writing occasionally, updates on my projects, book reviews, etc.

This is new to me, and I'm learning as I go, but I think it could be fun.

Unfortunately, I will need to limit the spots available to make sure I'm able to deliver at the level of quality my postal partners demand.

FAQ

What exactly will I receive each month?

At the very least, you will receive a written piece updating you on Harold and Edith. The story will flow in a mixture of novel chapters, letters, notes, etc. Some of them will be written by me about them, and some may be written from their perspective

Extra items may be included such as updates on my own writing process, thoughts about stories (movies / books), and hopefully someday some illustrations or simple merch related to Harold and Edith.

I will also do my best to make available an updated digital version of the story as it develops.

Are Harold and Edith open to receiving letters?

Yes! Please note that anything sent will be considered eligible for sending out through this same mail club, but it would be a joy for them to read and respond to correspondence.

How do I cancel my subscription?

All of the billing is handled through Posthouse. Please refer to your account there or navigate through your initial subscription email to cancel.

If you have any trouble beyond that, please email ericdavidsonwrites@gmail.com

Are Harold and Edith real?

Are you sure you want to know?

If someone is concerned, or needs the truth to be that they are not real, then I say to you now, "No, they are not real. I am a writer, and this is all part of a story I am working on. It is a fun way to interact with the story, and a fun way to interact with people who are interested in it. Please do not make serious decisions in your own life based on the advice of either myself, or the advice that I would offer through these characters. Be inspired to be imaginative, and learn to see characters in unexpected places."

Does that satisfy you?

Now, if you are reading, and you are no longer concerned, and you understand what I've said above and why I've said it, and you understand that there are no writer mice... then please think no more of it should you drive past one day and find me delivering cheese, scraps of paper, and fresh ink to my mailbox.

How long will the story be?

I don't know! I would love overtime to build enough material that it is full book-length, even if it is a mixture of perspectives and writing styles.

Will there be a digital or full version of the story available?

Digital version, yes. I would like (ideally) to keep updating an e-book format version of the story.

As far as a full printed version, I don't know. I would have to explore that later on. I am still working to become a published author, and I don't know what will happen once fame and riches changes me at my core (but I can promise you, Harold and Edith will be with me and working hard to keep me grounded)

My foolish hope is that there will be even a slight amount of love for their story, and I'll be able to work with a publisher to make it more available. No promises!

Chapter One

In which two mice move into a mailbox


It follows that an abandoned house would have an empty mailbox, but when they made their way inside, something had been left behind. It was a blue seed packet that read, “Forget-Me-Not”. Out of respect they tipped the pack outwards and let the seeds fall. So long had they been waiting that sprouts formed within minutes. Then, without any water and only an afternoon of sunshine, there were blue flowers blooming around the base of the post. The same day, if you had called someone over and pointed, they would have said, “Beautiful, aren’t they? They’ve been there as long as I can remember.”

The two that had found the seeds were mice, and they were writers. Good enough in fact to never waste a word they found. They had taken the name of the flowers from the packet and used a tad of sap to place the words on the door of their new home, having added under it, “nor will I forget you.” One of the mice, Harold, wrote the word “nor” whenever he had the opportunity, while his wife Edith’s favorite word was “together”. She often held it over him as being five letters longer and at least five times better, but then he would just chase her, chanting “nor” over and over again, until she brought a throwable object and his head “together”.

By the end of the first day, the final writing on the door read, “Forget-Me-Not, nor will I forget you. Together, remember?” They both loved the last word, and it might have even been truly each of their favorites, but that often slipped their mind.

How to explain the history of two writer mice? Long before the mailbox and the writing, they had met in the post office. Neither had planned to stay, nor had they intended to be writers, but meeting each other changed both of those intentions. Soon they each had more to say than they could and they turned to the volume of mail that passed through the station to pursue independent study, both learning to read in the hopes that they could satisfy their need to write. They learned quickly, much faster than usual given their romantic motivations, and quickly enough to realize that not once had they read anything sufficient for their own feelings.

One letter came through filled with such lovely intentions, but written so poorly that the two could not resist making some edits before resealing the letter and sending it along. After that they became distracted by their craft, and continued editing love letters as a form of delaying writing to one another and instead sprinkled their emotions among the communications in transit.

It did not take long for people to notice, and a time came when desperate lovers would travel great distances just to ensure that their romantic correspondence passed first through that town. Their efforts were public enough that there were always letters to work on, but remained private enough in the interest of the same lovers seeking assistance that they were never overwhelmed. Occasionally there were small notes of thanks included in the envelopes, or blank sheets of paper to supply the rewrites, or even money that the mice saved, just in case it ever became useful to them.

Rarely there were more specific requests spanning other genres, for example instructions to comb through a series of invoices to identify discrepancies. For all financial inquiries, Harold and Edith began rerouting to a town they believed housed an accounting fox and badger far more suited to the task.
